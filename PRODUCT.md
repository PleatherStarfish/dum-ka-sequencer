# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Caesura is for rhythm-focused composers, producers, live performers, and
technically fluent musicians who want a macOS MIDI instrument for probabilistic
Carnatic-inspired rhythmic structures.

Users may route generated MIDI into DAWs, Max, hardware synths, or the built-in
synth. They care about fast iteration on dense rhythmic material, reproducible
stochastic choices, and trust that the timeline preview and playback describe
the same realized score.

## Product Purpose

Caesura is a compact rhythm-tree MIDI sequencer for composing and performing
beat-based Carnatic-inspired probabilistic rhythm.

It helps users define cycles, beat-level gati, section boundaries, jathi accents,
Markov rhythm grouping, ratchet, pitch shaping, and channel hocket as musical
structure, then hear and inspect the exact realized result.

It is not a generic step sequencer, piano roll, DAW, notation system, sample
instrument, or groovebox clone. Its value is in making advanced rhythmic
structure playable, visible, repeatable, and trustworthy.

## Brand Personality

Minimalist, utilitarian, and instrument-like.

The product should feel precise, calm, dense, direct, and made for repeated
musical use. The interface should prioritize legibility, trust, and control over
decoration.

## Anti-references

- Generic DAW piano rolls.
- Conventional step sequencers.
- Glossy grooveboxes.
- Academic notation editors.
- Marketing dashboards.
- Cute or game-like synth toys.
- Decorative vaporwave aesthetics.
- Interfaces that make the timeline feel secondary to panels or spectacle.

## Design Principles

- Instrument first: Caesura should behave like a serious musical tool, not a
  demo surface or content site.
- Timeline as truth: the visible timeline and scheduled playback must come from
  the same score request.
- Show musical structure directly: central rhythmic choices should be visible
  and editable in or near the timeline.
- Preserve the model: gati subdivides each beat, boundaries occur after beats,
  fired boundaries start sections, rhythm grouping respects protected spans, and
  ratchet remains playback-only.
- Make stochastic behavior inspectable: probability, seed behavior, and realized
  choices should be clear enough that users can trust and repeat results.
- Keep controls compact and grouped by musical function.
- Maintain one controlling switch per playback feature; summaries should not
  become duplicate state.
- Prefer dense but readable layouts, stable dimensions, direct manipulation, and
  restrained visual hierarchy.
- Avoid decorative cards, ornamental effects, and UI that implies a different
  musical model than playback actually uses.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No explicit accessibility program or special accessibility requirements are
defined for the product at this stage.

Do not invent accessibility commitments in product guidance. Apply normal
platform and browser defaults, and avoid introducing unnecessary barriers as part
of ordinary interface craft.
